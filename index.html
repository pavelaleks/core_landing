How it works

Three steps — from a department archive to university revenue

  1. 1

    The university shares data

    Archives, text collections, speech recordings, field and lab observations. The university decides what can be offered and what cannot.

  2. 2

    We prepare it

    We clean and digitize it, mark up what matters together with the university's experts, check authors' rights and remove personal details.

  3. 3

    Companies pay — the university earns

    We sell the right to use the data, not the data itself — it stays the university's property. Up to 70% of revenue goes to the university, part of it to the authors.

Trust

How we protect the university, the authors and the buyer

Only with permission

We work only with data the university holds the rights to, and keep the paperwork for every set.

No personal data

Names, phone numbers and other personal details are removed before any sale. Storage follows Uzbekistan's Personal Data Law.

Authors get a share

We recommend universities pass at least 30% of their share to the authors, and we credit them in the data description.

Open stays open

We never lock up data that is already open. People pay for our work: checking, processing and updates.

No surveillance, no resale

Buyers may not identify people from the data, use it for surveillance or resell it.

Fully transparent

The university sees who bought its data and why, and can rule out certain kinds of buyers.

Questions

Frequently asked

What are “annotated data”?

Data in which people have marked what matters: place names, people, dates, what a document is about. That is how AI learns to understand a text rather than just see it. There is an example above — the Samarkand article about the Registan.

What can I buy today?

A pilot, custom data, clinic shortlists for medical tourism and a pre-order of the model check. Ready datasets from university archives will appear as agreements with universities are signed.

Why only universities, not individual researchers?

Data created at work usually belongs to the university, and research involving people needs ethics approval. When the university signs, the buyer gets clean rights and the researchers are protected and paid their share. A researcher can offer data through their university.

How are you different from open databases online?

Open databases host what is already published. We work with what isn't online — university archives — and add expert mark-up and rights checks.

How do you handle personal data?

We remove personal details before anything reaches a buyer and store data under Uzbekistan's Personal Data Law. Buyers may not try to identify people from the data.

Can a foreign company buy data?

Yes. We work under a contract with payment in US dollars, following Uzbekistan's currency rules.
