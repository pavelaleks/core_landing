We help universities earn from their research data — and AI developers get it legally

A university shares its archives, texts and research results with us. We clean them up, check the rights and license them to companies building artificial intelligence. They need knowledge about the region — from history and law to medicine, water and soils — and text in local languages. The university pays nothing and keeps 40–70% of the revenue.

How it works

Three steps — from a department archive to university revenue

  1. 1

    The university shares data

    Archives, text collections, speech recordings, field and lab observations. The university decides what can be offered and what cannot.

  2. 2

    We prepare it

    We clean and digitize it, mark up what matters together with the university's experts, check authors' rights and remove personal details.

  3. 3

    Companies pay — the university earns

    The university stays the owner; the buyer gets the files and the right to use them under a contract. The university keeps 70% of revenue if the data is ready as is, or 40–50% if we digitize and mark it up at our own cost. Part of the university's share goes to the authors.

Why it matters

AI barely knows Central Asia. University data can fix that.

01

AI gets our region wrong

Models learn mostly from English text, and there is little Uzbek, Kazakh, Kyrgyz or Tajik text online. So they get the region's history, laws and realities wrong more often. How often — our test will show.

02

The best data sits in universities

Decades of research sit in department archives and bring little benefit — to the university or to the researchers.

03

Companies need clean rights

Taking text from the internet without permission has become risky. Companies will pay for data that comes with paperwork.

Trust

How we protect the university, the authors and the buyer

Only with permission

We work only with data the university holds the rights to, and keep the paperwork for every set.

No personal data

Names, phone numbers and other personal details are removed before any sale. University data will be stored as Uzbekistan's Personal Data Law requires. Today we hold only open data.

Authors get a share

We recommend universities pass at least 30% of their share to the authors, and we credit them in the data description.

Open stays open

We never lock up data that is already open. People pay for our work: checking, processing and updates.

No surveillance, no resale

Buyers may not identify people from the data, use it for surveillance or resell it.

Fully transparent

The university sees who bought its data and why, and can rule out certain kinds of buyers.

Questions

Frequently asked

What are “annotated data”?

Data in which people have marked what matters: place names, people, dates, what a document is about. That is how AI learns to understand a text rather than just see it. There is an example above — the Samarkand article about the Registan.

What can I buy today?

Registries and shortlists (universities, clinics), analytics and reports, a pilot, custom data and a pre-order of the model check. Ready datasets from university archives will appear as agreements with universities are signed.

Why only universities, not individual researchers?

Data created at work usually belongs to the university, and research involving people needs ethics approval. When the university signs, the buyer gets clean rights and the researchers are protected and paid their share. A researcher can offer data through their university.

How are you different from open databases online?

Open databases host what is already published. We work with what isn't online — university archives — and add expert mark-up and rights checks.

How do you handle personal data?

We remove personal details before anything reaches a buyer. University data will be stored as Uzbekistan's Personal Data Law requires — on servers in Uzbekistan or where the law allows. Buyers may not try to identify people from the data.

Can a foreign company buy data?

Yes. We work under a contract with payment in US dollars, following Uzbekistan's currency rules.
